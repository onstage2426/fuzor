# Changelog

## 2.0.0 - unreleased

See `UPGRADE-2.0.md` for what calling code has to change.

Breaking:

- Index files written by 1.x (schema revisions 1 to 3) are no longer opened: `new Index()` throws a `QueryException` that says how to recreate the index. `Index::rebuild()` with a callback and a schema replaces such a file without opening it. The same happens for a file written by a newer version. 2.0 writes schema revision 4; the code that kept older revisions working (the narrow facet index, `strip_tags()` for old `stripHtml` files, defaults for missing `info` keys) is gone.
- `SchemaConfig::$facetFields` is replaced by `filterableAttributes` (fields for `filter`, `facets`, `distinct`, and `facetSearch()`) and `sortableAttributes` (fields for `sort`), and `searchableFields` is renamed `searchableAttributes`, as in Meilisearch. `$index->facetFields` is replaced by `$index->filterableAttributes` and `$index->sortableAttributes`, `$index->searchableFields` by `$index->searchableAttributes`.
- A field outside the matching list in `sort`, `filter`, `facets`, `distinct`, or a `FacetSearchQuery` now throws a `QueryException` naming the field and the declared fields, instead of being ignored with a warning. `$warnings` now only reports caps.
- Words in a quoted phrase always match exactly, as in Meilisearch: with `asYouType` the last word of a query was a prefix even inside closed quotes (`"blue jeans"` also found "jeanswear"). A query that ends in a quoted phrase has no prefix word, also when highlighting.
- A quoted phrase with a word that is in no document makes `search()` and `facetSearch()` return nothing, as in Meilisearch, where a phrase is never dropped from a query; it used to be ignored. `searchBoolean()` still leaves it out.
- `Config::$filterMaxDocs` is removed; it had no effect since 1.6.0.
- `Config::$fuzzyMinWordLength` is replaced by `Config::$typoTolerance` (`TypoTolerance`): `enabled`, `minWordSizeForOneTypo` (5), `minWordSizeForTwoTypos` (9, was fixed), `disableOnNumbers`, and `disableOnWords`.
- `SearchOptions::$matchingStrategy` (`MatchingStrategy::Last` default, `All`, `Frequency`): a multi-word `search()` returns documents containing the first word (`Last`), every word (`All`), or the rarest word (`Frequency`), ranked by how many words they match in that order. 1.x returned documents with any word. `sort` now applies after this words ranking instead of before it. `All` is exact when its rarest word is under `maxDocs`.
- `SearchOptions::$limit` defaults to 20 (was 100).
- New `Config::$maxTotalHits` (default 1,000), as in Meilisearch: no hit ranked past it can be paged to, and `$totalHits` stops at it, with the new `SearchResult::$totalHitsCapped` set. Also for a browse, whose total used to be the full count. Below the cap `search()` counts `$totalHits` exactly when words match more than `maxDocs` documents; it used to report the scored candidates (on the 44k catalog the median was a quarter of the real total), and a page past that number could still return hits. Cost on the 44k catalog: 3% on plain searches, 9% with a filter, about 2 ms with `MatchingStrategy::All`.
- Facet counts of a `search()` whose words match more than `maxDocs` documents are counted over every match (up to `maxFacetCountDocs`) instead of the scored candidates, which left them 76% off at the median on the 44k catalog. Such searches with three facets take about twice as long there (8 to 16 ms; with a filter 23 to 39 ms): counting costs about 1 µs per matching document.
- When words match more than `maxDocs` documents, the words bucket a page comes from is read and ranked by BM25 exactly while it holds at most 4 × `maxDocs` (2,000) documents, and the first word is read whole up to that size; the page used to come from each word's documents with the most occurrences, so a short document mentioning the words once was missed. On the 44k catalog 86% of first pages equal the uncapped ranking (was 50%), sorted 88% (72%); a plain search takes 13.2 ms instead of 7.1 ms, sorted 26 instead of 21, with a filter and facets 43 instead of 39. With a value-list filter on a field whose facet is counted, the other facets are counted in the same pass over the matches (filter and three facets 10% faster).
- When a page reaches the documents that contain only the first word (a one-word query, or other words that match nothing, as after many typos), it takes the first word's `maxDocs` documents with the best BM25 score, read in one sorted pass over the word, instead of those with the most occurrences. On the 44k catalog 89% of first pages equal the uncapped ranking (was 86%), with a typo 89% (67%), with a filter 83% (75%), page 10 84% (75%); queries with a typo take 8.6 ms instead of 5.8, with a filter and facets 52 instead of 45, plain searches 4% longer. Sorted searches are unchanged.
- With `distinct`, `search()` counts `$totalHits` over every match when words match more than `maxDocs` documents; it collapsed only the scored candidates (on the 44k catalog the total was exact for 45% of searches). Distinct searches take 57 ms instead of 41 there.
- `searchBoolean()` counts `$totalHits` over every match when a term matches more than `maxDocs` documents, also with `distinct`; it counted the candidates (on 300 searches of the 44k catalog the total was right for 18 with AND, 134 with OR, 19 with a negation). The page and the facet counts still come from the candidates. Boolean searches take 2.5–4.6 ms there instead of 1.1–1.3 ms.
- When words match more than `maxDocs` documents, `search()` pages past the ranked candidates are filled with the other matches that `$totalHits` counts (unscored, after the ranked ones); they were empty (3,000 matches, 24 per page: empty from page 22). With `sort`, those matches are sorted together with the candidates, so the first page can change too (a one-word sorted search 3.0 to 5.0 ms on the 44k catalog). `exhaustive` stays `false`. The same holds with `distinct`: its pages used to hold only the ranked candidates, so they ended before `$totalHits`, and a search whose filter passed none of the candidates returned nothing.
- The matches that fill a `search()` page past its ranked candidates come newest first (document ID descending, the order of a browse), and facet counts over more matches than `facetCountDocs` count the newest of them, in `search()`, `searchBoolean()` and a filtered browse (a browse whose matches are read from a numeric range filter counts those with the lowest values of that field). Both followed the order in which SQLite read the rows, which could differ between SQLite versions.
- A one-word `search()` whose filter matches far fewer documents than the word ranks the documents that pass the filter, so its page is the one the uncapped ranking gives; it ranked the word's best documents first and filtered them afterwards, which could leave the best matches out. Such searches are also faster.
- `FacetSearchQuery::$query` is renamed `$q`, as in Meilisearch.
- `QueryToken::$matchType` is a `MatchType` enum (`Exact`, `Prefix`, `Fuzzy`, `None`; string-backed with the former values) instead of a string.
- `Index` is `final` (and implements `\Countable`, so `count($index)` works). `SearchResult`, `IOException` and `QueryException` are `final` too.
- Facet values in `$facetDistribution` and `facetSearch()` are ordered alphabetically by default (`FacetOrder::Alpha`), as in Meilisearch; 1.x ordered them by count. Pass `FacetOrder::Count` to keep that order.
- A query word that exists in the index also matches its typos, as in Meilisearch (typo matching used to start only when the word had no match). Within a words bucket, documents that match with fewer typos rank first, before `sort`. On 1.43M Amazon products "bluetoth headphones" ("bluetoth" is in 20 titles) returns 792 hits instead of 20.
- A `language` no longer enables a stop word list, and the bundled lists are removed: every word is indexed and searched, as in Meilisearch. Stop words are the new `SchemaConfig::$stopWords` (single words, empty by default), left out of the index and of queries. The English list held 1,298 words, among them `c`, `men`, `tv`, `case` and `computer`: `"usb c cable"` was searched as `"usb cable"` and "Men" was never indexed. `Language::hasStopwords()` is removed, and the 27 language tags that only had a list (`af`, `cs`, `he`, `uk`, `vi`, …) throw a `QueryException` at index creation.
- `Config::$maxFacetCountDocs` defaults to 50,000 (was 10,000): facet counts over up to 50,000 matching documents are exact. A 28.6k-product category of 1.43M Amazon products with three facets counts all matches in 23 ms, where 1.7.1 took 35 ms for the first 10,000.
- `inspectQuery()` lists `-word` / `-"phrase"` exclusions in the new `QueryInspection::$negations` (with their normalised tokens and whether `search()` applies them) instead of among the searched tokens.
- String sort is case-insensitive: uppercase letters sort as if they were lowercase, so `"apple"` comes before `"Zebra"`. Accents are not folded. Sorting on string fields walks a new `sort_keys` table, written only for string values of sortable fields.
- `search()` and `facetSearch()` queries support exclusions: `shirt -formal` leaves out documents containing "formal", `-"long sleeve"` a phrase, and a query of only exclusions returns every other document. In 1.x a leading `-` was ignored and the word was searched for.
- `facetSearch()` matches `facetQuery` at the start of any word of a value and ignores case and Latin accents ("rosa cl" finds "rosa-clara" and "Rosa Clará"). It was a case-insensitive prefix of the whole value.
- Facet values with equal counts are always listed in value order, so the same request returns the same distribution whichever way it was counted (also which values survive the `maxValuesPerFacet` cut).
- Two swapped neighbouring characters count as one typo (`casaul` finds `casual`).
- Each field's word positions start on their own instead of continuing from the previous field: a quoted phrase no longer matches across the end of one field and the start of the next. Proximity scoring measures the distance between query words inside one field; a window whose words lie in different fields counts a fixed cross-field distance per word gap. On the 44k catalog 99% of 300 multi-word searches keep their top hit (mean top-24 overlap 0.98).
- A number as a filter value (`['stars' => 5]`, also in a list or `FacetExclude`) matches what its text matches (documents with `5`, `5.0` or `'5'`); it matched nothing, since values are stored as text. A value no document can hold (`true`/`false`, `null`, NaN, infinity, an object, a nested list) throws an `\InvalidArgumentException` instead of matching nothing. Same speed: the filter's SQL is the one its text gets.

Added:

- `Filter::all()`, `Filter::any()` and `Filter::not()` combine filter conditions with AND, OR and NOT, nested freely, wherever a filter map is accepted (`SearchOptions::$filter`, `FacetSearchQuery::$filter`, `deleteByFilter()`). An entry is a filter map with its usual meaning or another `Filter`; `not()` may wrap a group, and a document without the field passes `not()` of a condition on it. This covers two values of one key that must both be present (`Filter::all(['tags' => 'sale'], ['tags' => 'new'])`), an OR across keys and NOT around several conditions. `all()` without entries matches every document, `any()` without entries none. A filter map keeps its meaning and its speed, and `Filter::all($map)` gives the same result. Facet counts: a key with one condition of its own at the top level is counted without it, as with a map; values of one key combined by AND and keys inside a group are counted over the filtered result. Groups take the same paths as plain filters (a filtered browse or capped search reads each condition once when they fit in memory); on the 44k catalog with three facets (i5-9600K): an OR across two keys 4.3 ms, a key AND an OR group 9.0 ms, a negated group 8.2 ms, a search with an OR group 10.8 ms. Totals stay exact; 300 searches with an OR group gave the same first page as the uncapped ranking in 88% (a plain filter 85%), a negated group 97%.
- `SearchOptions::$showRankingScoreDetails` adds `_rankingScoreDetails` to each hit: the words bucket, typos, the value of each sort field and the BM25 score with its proximity factor that the hit was ranked by, in rank order. An empty query and `searchBoolean()` give the sort values only. Off by default.
- `SearchOptions::$facetStatsOnly` returns `facetStats` (min/max over the matches) for numeric filterable fields without counting their values, for a price slider's ends: the same stats as listing the field in `facets`, and no `facetDistribution` entry. The field's numeric index is read from both ends to the first match on each side; when the matching values sit in the middle of the range the field is counted with the facets as before. On a 7.7k-product shop the price bounds of the whole catalogue cost 0.3 ms instead of 3.9 ms, a search-bar request with a second facet 1.2–1.9 ms less; on 1.43M products the bounds of the whole index 0.6 instead of 15 ms; at most about 0.5 ms more than the facet where the walk gives up. Stats found this way cover every match, so they are exact where the facet's counts are approximate.
- `SearchOptions::$sortFacetValuesBy` (per field, `'*'` for the others) and `FacetSearchQuery::$sortFacetValuesBy` (`FacetOrder::Alpha` default, `Count`) order facet values; the order is applied before `maxValuesPerFacet` / `limit`.
- `$index->stopWords` lists the index's stop words (see `SchemaConfig::$stopWords` above). A stop word keeps its place between the other words, as in Meilisearch: the phrase `"bank of england"` matches "bank", any one word, "england", and `"bank england"` no longer matches "bank of england"; proximity counts it as a word between. `searchBoolean()` leaves them out of its expression as well (`bank of england` is `bank & england`, a trailing stop word makes the word before it the typed prefix); an AND with a stop word used to match nothing.
- `searchBoolean()` with quotes: `-"phrase"` (or `~"phrase"`) leaves out the documents with the phrase; it used to require the phrase, since the quotes were taken off before the `-` was read. `-(group)` negates the group like `~(group)`; the `-` was dropped. Operators inside quotes are words of the phrase (`"black or white"` was an OR). In `search()`, a `-` inside a quote pair (`"bank -" "england"`) no longer starts a negation with the next quote.
- A quote left open makes a phrase of the rest of the query, as if closed at the end (`blue "denim jacket`), in `search()`, `searchBoolean()`, `facetSearch()` and negations (`-"…`), as in Meilisearch; it was ignored. The words after it match exactly.
- `optimize(): int` purges the word index rows of deleted documents (see the `delete()` entry below) and returns how many documents it purged.
- `deleteByFilter(array $filter): int` deletes every document that matches a filter (the shape of `SearchOptions::$filter`, filterable fields only) and returns how many; an empty filter throws.
- `Index::remove($path)` deletes an index with everything Fuzor keeps next to it: the link at the path, every `{path}.v-{8 hex}` version, their `-wal`/`-shm` files, and temp files of builds that are no longer running, and returns the deleted paths. Unlinking the path alone, as before 1.7.1, leaves the version files behind.
- `Index::install($file, $path, check: false)` puts an index file made elsewhere (a snapshot from another server) at a path: copied next to it, synced, rejected unless it is a complete Fuzor index this version opens (optionally `PRAGMA quick_check`), then published like `rebuild()`. A file with a non-empty `-wal` is refused.
- A multi-document `insert()`, `update()` or `upsert()` tokenises and writes its documents in chunks of the new `Config::$insertChunkSize` (default 2,000) inside its single transaction, reading a generator one chunk at a time. On the 44k catalog, a streamed `insert()` peaks at 127 MB instead of 2 GB and `rebuild()` at about 150 MB instead of 2 GB, at the same speed. The `progress` callback's `$total` is `0` for a generator or other non-countable input.

Searches are slower because 1.7.1 ranked, counted and faceted only each word's first `maxDocs` documents: its pages, `totalHits` and facet counts were those of the candidates (on 300 searches 50% of first pages, 31% of totals were right, facet counts 76% off at the median), it scored about half of the candidates as if a word were missing, and it missed swapped-letter typos. 2.0 ranks the page's words bucket exactly and counts totals and facets over every match. Doing the same work (both versions with `maxDocs` uncapped), 2.0 is faster: two words 24 → 16 ms, a typo 40 → 22 ms, filter + sort + 6 facets 115 → 47 ms; prefix and swapped-letter searches take longer there (+7% to +87%), since every word also matches its typos. `insert()` and `rebuild()` index 50% more word rows because a language no longer enables a stop word list; with 1.7.1's English list as `stopWords` the same insert takes 27 s instead of 29–31 s, so the code itself is faster. `snapshotTo()` copies a 17% larger file (363 MB, the new facet and sort tables). `searchBoolean()` with a negation takes 0.8 → 1.0 ms, a word search sorted by a string field 1.3 → 2.5 ms, `facetSearch()` with a quoted phrase or an accented query and a filter 14–30% longer (they find more values). Every other read scenario is within ±7% or faster.

Further improvements (this version, relative to 2.0 milestone builds):

- Per-field term hit data moved into two columns on `doclist` (`field_id`, `field_split`); the separate `field_hits` table is gone. Index size −50 MB on the 44k catalog (319 → 269 MB). Field-boost pair read 1.3 → 0.9 ms.
- Partial-text `update()` / `upsert()` now rewrites only the word-index rows that actually changed. Price-only update of 200 products 358 → 46 ms; title + one word 9,541 → 2,888 WAL pages, 780 → 278 ms.
- Sorted, filtered browse pages choose between walking the sort field and sorting the matches by the expected walk length, and fall back to sorting when the walk runs long. On 1.43M products: a category sorted by price 33.6 → 17.8 ms, with a stars range sorted by reviews 73.8 → 32.2 ms, three categories 66.7 → 19.4 ms. Range filters count their rows from the facet counts (47 → 0.12 ms there).
- `delete()` removes a document's own rows at once and marks its rows in the word index deleted; they are purged in bulk by the new `optimize()`, by `rebuild()` / `clear()`, or by `delete()` itself once deleted documents reach 10% of the index. On the 44k catalog a 50-document delete takes 13 ms instead of 250–340 ms (WAL 541 pages instead of 5,335) and a single delete 0.5 ms instead of 3.4 ms. Until the purge, BM25's per-word document counts include the deleted documents (with 10% deleted: same first hit, 97% of top-20 sets unchanged) and searches add a 0.1–1.3 ms filter.
- `update()` and `upsert()` keep a document's word index entries when its searchable fields are unchanged (compared with the stored document, so only with the document store enabled) and rewrite only the stored document and its filterable / sortable values. On the 44k catalog a price-only update of 200 products takes 46 ms instead of 360 ms (WAL 1,046 pages instead of 8,284); an update that changes the titles costs the same as before.
- Facet counts over the whole index, and the counts of a page whose only filters are exclusions, read a new `facet_counts` table kept exact on every write, instead of counting every document. On the 44k catalog: facets of an unfiltered page 9.9 → 0.6 ms, price stats 6.2 → 1.3 ms, a shop page with five facets hiding 4,025 products 37 → 13 ms (hiding 34,409: 60 → 45 ms), an unrestricted `facetSearch()` 4.1 → 0.2 ms. Writes stay within a few percent.
- Filters on fields that never hold two values per document skip `DISTINCT` in their exact counts, and a range filter on the sort field of such a field seeks straight into the range: on the 44k catalog a price range sorted by price 11.5 → 0.8 ms, `gender: [Women, Men]` sorted by price 6.6 → 1.4 ms.
- Field boosts (`Config::$fieldBoosts`) read per-field hits only for the term and document pairs a search found, instead of every term against every candidate: with a title boost, `sh` 3.7 → 2.1 ms, "blue jeans" 7.2 → 5.3 ms, "blue cotton shirt" 13.5 → 9.5 ms.
- A filtered browse with facets reads its matching documents once for both the total and the facet counts, and text-only facets skip the numeric stats query. Exclusion-only facet counts on a large index subtract the excluded documents instead of scanning the kept ones whenever few are excluded: a shop page hiding 9% of a 533k catalog counts five facets in 171 ms instead of 714 ms.
- `update()` and `upsert()` write only the word index rows that change when a document's searchable text changes, also without a document store: appending one word to the title of 200 products writes 2,888 WAL pages instead of 9,541 and takes 278 ms instead of 780 ms on the 44k catalog; a document whose text changes completely is rewritten as before, at the same speed. Without a document store, an update that leaves the text unchanged no longer touches the word index.
- A browse filtered on one field whose documents hold one value each takes its total from the maintained facet counts instead of counting the matching rows: on the 44k catalog `gender: Men` sorted by price 0.79 → 0.29 ms (6.8 → 0.45 ms on a 533k catalog). Filter fields are also ordered by the same counts.
- A browse that combines filters with exclusions that select fewer documents than the filters counts its total as the filter's matches minus the excluded ones among them: men without sports products, five facets, 34 → 26 ms on the 44k catalog, 149 → 59 ms on 533k.
- A value list that selects most documents of a field every document has once (`gender: [Women, Men]`) is browsed as the exclusion of the other values when those hold at most `maxFacetCountDocs` documents. Its facet counts are then exact instead of approximate, and the 44k listing with six facets takes 13 ms instead of 21 ms.
- `distinct` and sorting over many matches read the field's values with one scan of the field instead of one lookup per document, and numbers sort without string keys: `distinct` browse 42 → 20 ms, with a filter and sort 55 → 44 ms.
- Exclusion-only facet counts subtract the excluded documents' counts whatever share of the index they exclude (was up to 25%, then a scan): `-shirt` with three facets 33 → 11 ms on the 44k catalog.
- Filtered facet counts read one row per matching document for all facet fields instead of one row per document and field: each document's filterable values are stored together as packed IDs in a new `doc_facets` table (with a `facet_value_ids` dictionary). Same counts, about 0.6–1 µs per counted document. On the 44k catalog, gender Men with six facets 42.6 → 9.9 ms, Tshirts with six facets 31.6 → 8.1 ms; on 1.43M Amazon products, a 28.6k category with three facets 34.5 → 10.8 ms. Single-document updates cost up to 0.2 ms more, a full build 3–5% more, and the 1.43M index is 70 MB larger. Only the counted values of the requested facets are looked up, so a facet with a value per document (a price) no longer slows every request down.
- `facetSearch()` counts the values of a filtered or queried set in PHP instead of grouping them in SQL, from the documents' packed facet rows: 15–36% faster with a filter or a broad query (a 28.6k category of 1.43M products, stars: 23.7 → 15.2 ms).
- A multi-word `search()` no longer loses documents that contain every word when each word is common. Each word still fetches its `maxDocs` best rows first; when those hold fewer full matches than the page needs (or with a filter, `-word`, `distinct` or `sort`), the documents containing all words (then all but the last, …) are read exactly while the rarest word is in at most 50,000 documents. A bucket of more than 500 documents scores the 500 with the most hits of its rarest word and ranks the others after them (`exhaustive: false`). On 1.43M Amazon products "women cotton dress" returns 20 of 20 full matches instead of 1 (22 → 39 ms), `women "cotton dress"` 6 hits instead of 0. Completing a word for the candidates looks each document up instead of scanning the word's postings, so most multi-word searches got faster: "men running shoes" 19 → 10 ms, "women shoes" 20 → 8 ms, `"usb c cable"` 29 → 20 ms.
- A sorted multi-word `search()` returns the first documents in sort order among all that contain every word, not only among each word's `maxDocs` best rows. Before, "black jean" sorted by price on 44k products returned 1 of the 20 cheapest full matches; it now takes 3 → 13 ms (1.43M Amazon products: "men running shoes" by price 5 → 47 ms, "bluetooth headphones" 4 → 23 ms).
- Proximity ranking runs after filtering and stops once the requested page is settled (with sort fields it only breaks ties on the page), so filtered and sorted multi-word searches no longer compute word distances for documents that are filtered out or never shown.
- Keyword searches spend less time in SQL with the same results (pages, totals, facet counts and ranking details identical on 8,706 checked requests over the 44k catalog). BM25 parameters are converted to numbers once per query instead of on every row (PDO binds floats as text), the documents containing all words are intersected in PHP and read once, words are completed for many documents by reading their rows in order, filters and negated words are checked on the candidates more cheaply, and distinct totals and single-valued sort fields read their values in one pass. Measured on the i5-9600K benchmark server, 300 searches per option set: 12–31% faster per set (plain 7.3 → 5.6 ms, sort 14.0 → 9.7, filter + facets 23.9 → 19.2, distinct 28.3 → 21.0); single requests: filter + sort + 6 facets 58.9 → 38.3 ms, "blue cotton shirt" 23.5 → 13.4, `sh` 24.5 → 15.8, `casual shirt -formal` 44.7 → 24.7, distinct browse with a filter and sort 50.6 → 34.6. `searchBoolean()` and browse pages are unchanged. Later (same server, same checks): the facet counts of such a search skip the per-document probe of an excluded value and the duplicate removal when the result set cannot exceed `maxFacetCountDocs`, cut words are completed from a narrower index, and `facetSearch()` reads a query's words without sorting them when none can be cut; a shop search with an exclusion and 5 facets 20.1 → 15.2 ms, "black je" 11.0 → 8.4, `facetSearch()` with a query 10.5 → 8.4 and with a quoted phrase 21.1 → 17.6, every option set 2–5% faster. Then: quoted phrases are matched from the positions of their rarest word when that reads fewer rows (the total of `"blue jeans"` 16.7 → 11.3 ms, 300 quoted searches 8% faster, `facetSearch()` with quoted phrases 14%), and a browse counts each filtered facet straight from the filter's SQL, with the other facets in the same pass (two filters + 3 facets 22.8 → 19.5 ms).
- Shop pages are faster with the same results, measured on a 7.7k-product shop (17 page types, 40 filterable fields, field boosts, a constant `FacetExclude`, highlighting): 182 → 129 ms for all of them together, an archive page 11.1 → 7.0 ms, a two-word search 38.8 → 30.5 ms. Field boosts are applied after the filters and read a term's rows in sequence when a query covers most of them, facet counts under an exclusion take one pass per key, and cropping no longer tokenises the text when there is no query. Counting facets from packed value IDs then skips a merge in the usual single batch: another 4% on those pages (125 → 120 ms in the same run).
- Filtered shop pages and search-as-you-type are faster with the same results (a 7.7k-product shop, 106 page types, i5-9600K): a filtered browse reads each filter's documents once and counts each document's facet values once, however many facet groups the page counts, and a search whose words are cut at `maxDocs` gets its facets and total from the same read. Category + three sidebar filters 7.2 → 4.1 ms, shop + two filters 13.0 → 7.9, a multi-value filter 18.1 → 10.9, a count next to a filter 2.2 → 1.1, the search bar's first letters 44–47 → 22–24 ms, a two-word search 35 → 31; all 106 pages 1,700 → 1,428 ms. Unfiltered pages, single filters and uncapped searches are unchanged; the 44k and 1.43M catalogues within noise.
- A faceted search whose words are cut at `candidatesPerWord` takes its total from the facet count's read of the result set when that read covers every match, instead of reading the result set a second time to count it. Same results; on 1.43M products "black" in a 28.6k category with one facet 195 → 122 ms, three words 236 → 166 ms (i5-9600K).
- A filtered browse counts each condition's rows once per request; a range on a key with a value per document (a price) was summed up to three times. On 100k products a wide price range next to another filter, without facets, 15.8 → 11.6 ms, back to its time before the browse facet change above (category + range 17.2 → 12.9 ms). The facet counts of a filtered browse check excluded values on each document's facet row instead of probing every document: a category counted over a price range minus hidden products (47.7k documents) 121 → 82 ms, a page with an exclusion, two filters and 5 facets 150 → 103 ms; same counts (where a count reaches `facetCountDocs` it is taken the old way).

Fixed:

- `_formatted` now marks words that matched through a typo or a synonym, as Meilisearch does, and a crop centres on them; before, a search for `terrakotta` found "terracotta" and left it unmarked. Searches with such matches cost about 0.5 ms more on a 7.7k-product shop.
- A crop could lose the word it was made for: the excerpt ended on the match, and the cut to `cropLength` could remove it. The match now sits in the middle of the excerpt (languages with n-grams are unchanged).
- Paging through a multi-word search without filters or sort could show a document on two pages and leave others out, when a words bucket held more than 4 × `maxDocs` documents (3,000 matches: 594 repeats). The same documents are now ranked on every page. This also makes first pages more exact (300 ecom searches, pages equal to the uncapped ranking: 89 → 93%) and costs those searches about 18% (5.6 → 6.6 ms), more at 1.43M products when the bucket is large ("bluetooth headphones" 11 → 20 ms, others unchanged); searches with filters, negated words, `distinct` or sort are unchanged.
- A quoted phrase in a large words bucket: members that do not contain the phrase no longer take the place of phrase matches among the 4 × `candidatesPerWord` documents ranked exactly, so every phrase match up to that number is scored (300 ecom phrase searches, pages equal to the uncapped ranking 95 → 99%, 7.1 → 5.9 ms; "blue jeans" 20.6 → 13.7 ms, now `exhaustive`).
- A words bucket above 4 × `candidatesPerWord` now scores the members in which its rarest word weighs most (BM25 term frequency: hits against document length) instead of those with the most hits, which favoured long documents (300 ecom searches, pages equal to the uncapped ranking: plain 93 → 97%, page 10 85 → 89%, `distinct` 79 → 84%, filter 83 → 85%, same average time; "casual shirt" and "blue cotton shirt" now give the uncapped first page, +1–2 ms each for the length read).
- `Config::$maxDocs`, `$maxFacetCountDocs` and `$maxTotalHits` moved into a `Limits` value object, `Config::$limits`: `candidatesPerWord`, `facetCountDocs`, `totalHits` (same defaults). Presets `Limits::fast()` (the default) and `Limits::exact()` (every word read whole and facet counts over every match, so results are exact; `totalHits` stays 1,000 unless passed). Each value must be at least 1. Warnings name the new settings. See UPGRADE-2.0.md.
- A typo'd word could miss its match when more than `fuzzyMaxExpansions` (50) terms shared its first three letters, because the candidates were cut by frequency before their distance was checked. The cap now applies to the closest matches.
- In a multi-word `search()` where a word matches more than `Config::$maxDocs` documents, documents found through another word were scored and ranked as if they lacked the common word, so documents matching all words could rank below documents matching one. Those documents are now scored for every word they contain ("casual shirt" on the 44k catalog: about half of the candidates gained the missing word).
- A prefix whose expansions together count more documents than the index (`car` matching `cara`, `carb`, `carc`) got a negative IDF, which ranked documents with more matching words last.
- After a write that failed and was rolled back (an `insert()` with a duplicate ID in a later chunk), the next writes on the same `Index` reused cached IDs of words, facet fields and field names that the rollback had removed, and the cached document count stayed raised: those documents could not be found by search or filter, also not from other connections.
- `has()` on an existing document, a filtered browse, and `distinct` or sorting over 1,000 or more documents left a read open on the connection until its next query. Meanwhile another connection's `checkpoint()` or `close()` waited for the busy timeout (5 s by default), and the write-ahead log of a long-lived reader could not be reset.
- With PHP's `open_basedir` set, a read-only `Index` and `Index::remove()` failed on every file: they opened it as an SQLite `file:` URI (`?mode=ro`), which PHP refuses under `open_basedir`. `remove()` reported a valid index as "Not a Fuzor index, refusing to delete it". Read-only files are now opened with `Pdo\Sqlite::OPEN_READONLY`, and `remove()` refuses only a file that is not an SQLite database or lacks Fuzor's tables; a file it cannot read throws an `IOException` with the reason.
- In a multi-word `search()`, a document found through one term of a word that matches several terms (a typo, a prefix expansion or a synonym) did not get its other terms of that word when they were past `Config::$maxDocs`: it scored too low, or ranked as a typo match although it contained the word itself, while the result claimed to be exhaustive. Plain searches on the 44k catalog take about 1 ms longer (6.2 to 7.2 ms).
- A multi-word `search()` whose words each match more than `Config::$maxDocs` documents left out the documents that contain a word only through a typo when it read the full matches exactly (with `MatchingStrategy::All`, a filter or a sort), while reporting the result as exhaustive: `All` "classic slip grey" on the 44k catalog found 33 of 39. Filtered searches there take about 10% longer.
- `searchBoolean()` with a negation (`a -b`, `-b a`, `a ~(b | c)`) returned documents that contain the negated word when that word is in more than `Config::$maxDocs` documents: only its first `maxDocs` documents were subtracted. The documents of the other side are now checked for the negated word or expression exactly. On 600 such queries over the 44k catalog, 333 had such hits (6,029 in total) and now none. A negated word above `maxDocs` no longer makes the result approximate by itself.
- `searchBoolean()` with `asYouType: false` expanded a negated last word as a prefix: `sedan -merc` left out documents with "mercedes".
- `Index::exists()` opens the file read-only, so it can no longer create an empty database when the file, or the target of a symlink at the path, is removed while it checks.

Faster, same results:

- A search for a common word next to a selective filter or quoted phrase reads its exact total, its facet counts and its later pages from that filter or phrase instead of from every document of the word.
- An unfiltered facet over a field of numbers (a price) reads only the values it returns; other unfiltered facets sort their values once instead of twice.
- A browse that excludes all but a few values of a field counts the kept documents instead of subtracting the excluded ones.
- A numeric range on a field with many distinct values is counted along the numeric index instead of over every value of the field.
- A browse facet next to an excluded value and a filter matching more than `facetCountDocs` documents is read once instead of twice.
- A numeric range on a field where several documents share each of many values (a price) is counted from the values inside the range, and a sort or `distinct` on a field with many values no longer adds up the field's rows first. Indexes built with an earlier 2.0 development version have to be rebuilt.

## 1.7.1 - 2026-10-07

Fixed:

- `rebuild()` could pair the new index file with the old file's write-ahead log. SQLite finds a database's `-wal` by file name, so when another process still had the old file open for writing, its uncheckpointed commits — and every commit it made after the swap — were read as pages of the new file: old documents showing up in the rebuilt index, or a corrupt file. `snapshotTo()` deleted the old sidecars after its rename, which left a short window for the same problem. Both now publish each new file under a name of its own, `{path}.v-{8 hex}`, and atomically swap a symlink at `{path}` to it, so two files never share a `-wal`. The previous version is kept until the next publish. Copy or back up the index with a tool that follows symlinks. See "Index files after a rebuild or snapshot" in `docs/indexing.md`.
- `new Index($path, force: true)` over an existing index deleted the old file and its `-wal`/`-shm` and created the new file under the same name, which left a moment in which another process's connection could pair the new file with the old sidecars. It now publishes the empty index the same way as `rebuild()`.

## 1.7.0 — 2026-09-27

Added:

- `FacetExclude` — exclusion filters for `search()`, `searchBoolean()`, browse, and `facetSearch()`. Wrap any filter value (a value, a list of values, or a `FacetRange`) to keep only the documents that do not match it: `filter: ['visibility' => new FacetExclude('exclude-from-search')]`. Documents without the field pass, and a multi-value document is removed as soon as one of its values matches, which positive filters cannot express. Exclusions combine with other filters by AND and also apply when their own field is counted: they are constraints, not selections, so they are never counted disjunctively. An exclusion on an undeclared field matches nothing and adds a warning, like any undeclared filter. See "Excluding values" in `docs/search.md`.
- A browse or `facetSearch()` whose only filters are exclusions is answered from the excluded documents instead of visiting every kept one, so its facet counts are exact at any index size and its cost follows the number of excluded documents. On the 44k catalog, a page with its total takes under 1 ms, and three facets take 36–52 ms with 410–4,000 products excluded (33 ms unfiltered).
- `HtmlText::toText()` is now public: the HTML-to-visible-text conversion that `stripHtml` uses for indexing. To have the document store (and so hits, `get()`, and `stream()`) hold text instead of HTML, convert the fields with it before inserting; see "Storing text instead of HTML" in `docs/indexing.md`. The conversion is not idempotent, so convert once from the original HTML and do not combine a pre-converted field with `stripHtml`.
- `docs/search.md` explains how to sort by the document ID: declare `id` in `facetFields`. The 1.6.0 notes below now mention that an undeclared `id:asc` was among the sort specs that stopped working.

Fixed:

- `facetStats` ignored a bound of exactly `0` whenever a facet field was counted with a per-field scan — always for counts over the whole index (such as an unfiltered browse), and for a single facet counted over more than 2,000 matching documents. A `price` field with free items reported the lowest non-zero price as `min`, and a field whose values are all `<= 0` reported the wrong `max`.

## 1.6.0 — 2026-09-26

Behaviour changes to check when upgrading:

- String facet values now sort strictly by bytes. Numeric-looking strings (`"10"`, `"9"`) used to compare as numbers against each other, which made mixed sets order inconsistently; they now sort as text like any other string, as in Meilisearch. Index numbers as `int`/`float` to sort them numerically (range filters and `facetStats` already require that).
- A `sort` spec on a field that is not a declared facet field is now ignored (with a warning), so the query keeps its normal order. Previously every document tied on it, which on a browse replaced the documented newest-first order with oldest-first. This includes `id`: `id:asc` only sorted by ascending ID because of that tie. To sort by the document ID, declare `id` in `facetFields`.
- A browse with a filter now reports the exact `totalHits`; on large indexes this can be much higher than before (see the browse fix below).
- `stripHtml` indexing changed in schema revision 3. Existing `stripHtml` indexes keep the old tokenisation until `Index::rebuild()`.

Added:

- `SearchResult::$warnings` / `getWarnings()` and `FacetSearchResult::$warnings` / `getWarnings()` — human-readable notices about options that were ignored or had no effect, such as a `sort`, `filter`, `facets`, `distinct`, or `facetName` field that is not a declared facet field, and about caps that were hit. Undeclared filters still match nothing.
- `SearchResult::$exhaustive` / `isExhaustive()`, `SearchResult::$approximateFacets` / `getApproximateFacets()`, and `FacetSearchResult::$exhaustive` / `isExhaustive()` — say when a cap made a result approximate: a keyword above `Config::$maxDocs`, a prefix expanding to more than `Config::$fuzzyMaxExpansions` terms, or facet counts over more than `Config::$maxFacetCountDocs` documents. Detection costs one extra row per capped lookup.
- `SearchOptions::$escapeFormatted` (default `false`) — makes every `_formatted` value safe HTML: stored text is escaped, the highlight tags are inserted verbatim, and on a `stripHtml` index the stored HTML is converted to its visible text first, so highlighting no longer lands inside tags or attributes and cropping no longer cuts through markup. `Highlighter`, `Snippeter`, `Index::highlighter()`, and `Index::snippeter()` gained a matching `escape` parameter. The default output is unchanged; `docs/formatting.md` now states that it is only safe to render when the stored text is trusted.
- `Tokenizer::sortKey()` — lowercases, folds Latin accents (`é` → `e`, `ß` → `ss`), and collapses whitespace for a human-facing A–Z sort field such as `title_sort`. Uses a built-in table, so keys are identical on every server.
- `Index::cleanupTempFiles(string $path, int $minAgeSeconds = 3600)` — removes the `{path}.tmp-*` files (and their `-wal` / `-shm`) that a `rebuild()` or `snapshotTo()` leaves behind when its process is killed mid-build, and returns the deleted paths. `rebuild()` now sweeps them before it starts, as `snapshotTo()` already did.
- `docs/indexing.md` documents that writes made to an index while `rebuild()` runs are lost at the swap, and how to serialize or replay them.

Fixed:

- A browse (empty query) with a filter, sort, facets, or distinct only considered the newest `max(filterMaxDocs, maxFacetCountDocs)` documents (10,000 by default). On a larger index, sorted pages missed older documents and filtered `totalHits` undercounted — on a 44k-document catalog, a `gender` filter reported 2,907 hits instead of 22,160. Browse is now answered by SQL over the whole index: totals, pages, and sort order are exact, and a sorted page walks the sort field's index and stops once the page is full. On that catalog, sorted and filtered pages take 0.1–3 ms (previously 12–122 ms). Unfiltered facet counts are exact; filtered ones stay capped at `maxFacetCountDocs` as in `search()`.
- `SchemaConfig::$stripHtml` now converts HTML to the visible text before indexing. Plain `strip_tags()` glued the words of adjacent blocks together (`<p>foo</p><p>bar</p>` was indexed as `foobar`), indexed `<script>`/`<style>` contents, and indexed entities as words (`&amp;` as `amp`). Block tags now separate words, hidden elements are dropped, and entities are decoded. This is schema revision 3 (`Index::CURRENT_SCHEMA_VERSION`); indexes without `stripHtml` are unaffected and already report revision 3.
- Sort order is now a strict total order, independent of the order candidates arrive in: numbers before strings in both directions, numbers by value, strings by bytes, missing values last.
- A multi-value facet field now sorts by the document's smallest value ascending and largest descending, and `distinct` groups it by its smallest value. Previously an arbitrary value was used.
- `facetSearch()` with a `filter` and no `query` only counted the first `filterMaxDocs` (2,000) matching documents; it is now exact.
- `facetSearch()` with a `query` that matched nothing and a `filter` returned counts as if there were no query; it now returns no values.
- `searchBoolean()` returned no results when operators had spaces around them (`shirt | jeans`, `shirt & jeans`), when words were separated by more than one space, or when the query ended in a space — the parser turned every space into an AND, producing dangling operators. Spacing no longer changes the meaning, dangling operators and lone symbols are dropped, parentheses are balanced, NOT works on either side of an AND (`-jeans shirt`), a word followed by a group is an AND (`shirt (jeans or blue)` used to behave like an OR), and a query that is only negations returns no results instead of failing.
- `snapshotTo()` deleted every `{path}.tmp-*` file before starting, including the temp files of a `rebuild()` or `snapshotTo()` still running in another process, which could make that run's final rename fail. Each build now holds an `flock()` on a `{tmp}.lock` file, and the sweep only removes groups whose lock is free and whose newest file is at least an hour old.

Performance and deprecations:

- Faceted search counts two or more facet keys over large result sets up to ~30% faster on schema revision 2+ files.
- Sorting search results by facet fields ranks each field's distinct values once and orders candidates with one native `array_multisort()` instead of a PHP comparison callback — a sorted, faceted search on the 44k catalog went from 15.9 ms to 14.2 ms.
- `facetSearch()` with a `query` or `filter` counts values by looking up each matching document instead of scanning every value of the facet — 10–20% faster for broad restrictions, far faster for narrow ones.
- `Config::$filterMaxDocs` no longer has any effect, since facet filters are always evaluated exactly. It is still accepted and will be removed in 2.0.
